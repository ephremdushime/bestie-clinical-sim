# Bestie Clinical Sim — University Pilot Pitch Brief

## One-line proposition
Bestie Clinical Sim is an AI-supported clinical education platform where health-professions students practise complete, fictional patient encounters, receive structured formative feedback, and collaborate on assessment, management, escalation, and referral—before facing real clinical situations.

## The problem
Clinical competence requires more than remembering facts. Students need repeated opportunities to practise patient communication, structured history-taking, clinical reasoning, investigation selection, safe management, teamwork, and recognizing when to seek help or refer. Access to consistent, repeatable practice and timely individualized feedback can be limited by clinical workload, patient availability, and educator time. These claims should be validated locally rather than presented as quantified findings without evidence.

## Proposed solution
- Interactive virtual patients with consistent case facts and branching responses.
- Structured investigation and management decisions.
- Emphasis on safety, ethics, empathy, professionalism, escalation, referral, and handover.
- Clear-English patient interaction designed for multilingual learning environments.
- Collaborative rooms for team-based cases.
- Educator review dashboard and formative feedback.
- Roadmap from web simulation to voice, then VR/AR environments.

## First pilot scope
One draft adult acute-respiratory case, one case engine, formative checklist, browser speech support, collaborative text room, and educator activity dashboard. The case/rubric are not clinically approved yet and must be reviewed by faculty before learner use.

## What we request from the University
1. A faculty sponsor and clinical educator working group.
2. Review of the first case, rubric, referral expectations, and local protocols.
3. A small supervised usability pilot after required institutional approvals.
4. Advice on curriculum alignment and the most valuable first specialty.
5. A review meeting to decide whether to expand to pediatrics, obstetrics/gynaecology, emergency medicine, or orthopedics.

## Pilot success indicators
- Students can complete a case without facilitator technical help.
- Patient responses remain consistent with approved case facts.
- Educators find the report interpretable and clinically appropriate.
- Critical safety omissions are surfaced for educator review.
- Students and educators report useful learning value and identify clear improvements.
- No real patient-identifiable data is entered; access and data handling requirements are followed.

## Safety, governance, and honesty
The platform is for education only. Automated scores are formative and not validated for progression decisions. AI responses can be wrong. The first case must be reviewed and approved by qualified clinicians. Research involving students or educational outcomes requires the applicable institutional review and consent process. The initial pilot should use fictional data and controlled access.

## 90-second pitch
Medical students need opportunities to practise the whole clinical encounter—not only answer multiple-choice questions. They need to listen to patients, ask focused questions, interpret investigations, make safe decisions, communicate with colleagues, and recognize when to escalate or refer. Bestie Clinical Sim is being developed to provide repeatable, interactive patient simulations with structured feedback. We are starting with a web-based prototype and a draft acute-respiratory case, then building toward voice interaction and real-time teamwork. Our priority is not to replace clinical placements or educators; it is to give students an additional safe practice environment and give educators a structured way to review performance. We are seeking a faculty sponsor to help validate the case and rubric, identify curriculum fit, and guide a small supervised usability pilot. We will measure usability and clinical-review agreement, keep the first cases fictional, and not use automated scores for high-stakes decisions. With University guidance, we can build a locally relevant tool that supports clinical reasoning, professionalism, teamwork, and safe referral.
